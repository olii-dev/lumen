smaller is a trade-off, not lossless magic. start high for music, compare, then lower if needed. Opus uses variable bitrate and usually offers a better size/quality trade-off; MP3 plays in more apps. re-encoding already-compressed audio can reduce quality, even at a higher bitrate. originals stay untouched. a larger result is clearly flagged. exports remove tags and artwork; no loudness changes or silence trimming. MP3 uses joint stereo, a higher-effort encoder and a single resampling pass; WAV dither trades a tiny noise floor for less quantisation distortion.

limits: 100 files per batch, 80 MB each, 500 MB total input, 512 MB total results, 10 minutes per file. mono and stereo only. files are decoded and encoded one at a time to keep memory bounded. Opus is 48 kHz; MP3 keeps the selected 44.1/48 kHz rate. playback/decoding support varies by browser.
